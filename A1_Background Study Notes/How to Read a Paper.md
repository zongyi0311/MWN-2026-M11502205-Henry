# Notes: How to Read a Paper

**Author:** S. Keshav  
**Main idea:** Research papers should be read in up to three passes. Each pass has a different goal, so readers can avoid wasting time on unnecessary details.

## 1. The Three-Pass Method

### First Pass: Get the Big Picture

**Time:** About 5–10 minutes

Read:

- The title, abstract, and introduction
- Section and subsection headings
- The conclusion
- The references, especially familiar papers

At the end of the first pass, answer the **Five Cs**:

1. **Category:** What type of paper is this?
2. **Context:** How is it related to other research?
3. **Correctness:** Do the assumptions seem valid?
4. **Contributions:** What are the main contributions?
5. **Clarity:** Is the paper well written and easy to understand?

The first pass helps decide whether the paper is worth reading in more depth.

### Second Pass: Understand the Main Content

**Time:** Up to 1 hour

Read more carefully, but skip detailed proofs and complicated technical steps.

Focus on:

- Figures, diagrams, and illustrations
- Graph labels, axes, and error bars
- The main argument and supporting evidence
- Important references that you have not read yet

Take brief notes or write comments in the margins.

At the end of the second pass, you should be able to explain the paper’s main idea and evidence to another person.

If the paper is still difficult, you can:

- Set it aside
- Read background material first
- Return to it later
- Continue to the third pass

### Third Pass: Understand the Paper in Depth

**Time:** About 4–5 hours for beginners; about 1 hour for experienced readers

Try to virtually re-implement the paper. In other words, reconstruct the work using the authors’ assumptions and methods.

During this pass:

- Examine every assumption
- Challenge each important statement
- Reconstruct the paper’s logic and structure
- Compare your reasoning with the authors’ approach
- Identify hidden weaknesses and limitations
- Note possible ideas for future research

At the end, you should be able to remember the paper’s overall structure and identify its strengths, weaknesses, missing citations, implicit assumptions, and possible problems in its experiments or analysis.

## 2. How to Conduct a Literature Survey

### Step 1: Find Initial Papers

Use an academic search engine such as Google Scholar. Search with carefully chosen keywords and find three to five recent papers.

Read each paper using the first pass, then examine their related work sections. This may lead you to a useful survey paper.

### Step 2: Find Important Researchers and Papers

Look for:

- Frequently cited papers
- Repeated author names
- Important researchers in the field

Read the key papers and visit the researchers’ websites to find their recent publications.

### Step 3: Check Major Conferences

Visit the websites of important conferences and scan their recent proceedings.

Read the most relevant papers using at least two passes. If these papers repeatedly cite another important paper, find it and continue the process.

## 3. Why This Method Is Useful

The three-pass method:

- Prevents the reader from getting lost in details too early
- Saves time
- Helps estimate how much effort a paper requires
- Allows different levels of reading depending on the purpose
- Works for reviews, background research, class assignments, and literature surveys

## Summary

The most important lesson is that reading a research paper does not mean reading every sentence with the same level of attention. I should first understand the paper’s purpose and contribution, then decide whether deeper reading is necessary.

For future papers, I will begin with the first pass and answer the Five Cs. If the paper is relevant, I will continue to the second pass and summarize the main argument. I will use the third pass only when I need a deep understanding, such as when reviewing a paper or building on its research.
